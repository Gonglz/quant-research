# Four-Week Minimum Foundations

Goal: build enough economics, finance, accounting, and statistics to formulate and test quantitative research questions. This is not a comprehensive academic curriculum.

## Operating rule

Use roughly 20% of time on foundations and 80% on question-driven learning after this phase. Each study day should end with:

1. What mechanism did I learn?
2. Which variables can observe it?
3. When might the mechanism fail?

## Week 1 — Economic system

1. Supply, demand, margin, opportunity cost, elasticity.
2. Fixed/variable/marginal cost and operating leverage.
3. Competition, barriers to entry, pricing power.
4. Real/nominal GDP, CPI/core CPI, unemployment.
5. Fed funds, nominal/real rates, inflation expectations, monetary transmission.
6. Build Macro Map v0.1; no new theory.

Primary objective: explain how shocks propagate through prices and quantities.

## Week 2 — Company economics and valuation foundations

1. Income statement, balance sheet, cash-flow statement.
2. D&A, capex, working capital, free cash flow.
3. ROE, ROIC, WACC, reinvestment.
4. Present value, future value, NPV, discount rates.
5. DCF mechanics and sensitivity to growth/discount rates.
6. Decompose one familiar company into revenue, cost, capital intensity, reinvestment, and FCF drivers.

Primary objective: connect industry economics to cash flow and intrinsic value.

## Week 3 — Asset pricing foundations

1. Equity as discounted future cash flow.
2. Bonds: coupon, yield, maturity, Treasury, spread, duration intuition.
3. Why real yields affect long-duration equities.
4. Risk-free rate and risk premia.
5. P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/B, FCF yield in context of growth, ROIC, risk, and duration.
6. Build a transmission map separating discount-rate effects from cash-flow effects.

Primary objective: stop treating market narratives as one-variable rules.

## Week 4 — Empirical discipline

1. Mean, variance, standard deviation, percentiles, skew, fat tails.
2. Correlation and confounding.
3. Simple regression: alpha, beta, residual, R-squared.
4. Statistical vs economic significance; confidence intervals and sample size.
5. Time-series traps: autocorrelation, trend, non-stationarity, look-ahead, survivorship, data snooping, regime change.
6. First full experiment: real yield vs QQQ, then condition on inflation/growth regimes.

Primary objective: distinguish a plausible story from evidence.

## Deliberately deferred

Do not prioritize DSGE, advanced micro theory, stochastic calculus, full Black-Scholes derivations, VAR/VECM/GARCH, advanced econometrics, ML factor mining, HFT, or complex portfolio optimization during this phase.

## Promotion rule

Research findings stay here by default. A transformation should move toward Quant-v2 only when it is reusable, clearly defined, economically motivated, and sufficiently validated. Missing trustworthy source data should become a Quant-v2 data request rather than ad-hoc ingestion inside this repo.
