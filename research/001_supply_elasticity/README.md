# 001 — Supply Elasticity

## Question

Why can a small change in commodity demand produce a much larger change in price?

## Minimum concepts

- Supply and demand
- Market equilibrium
- Marginal adjustment
- Short-run vs long-run elasticity
- Inventory as a buffer

## Working mechanism

```text
Demand shock
    -> physical supply cannot adjust quickly
    -> short-run supply elasticity is low
    -> inventories absorb part of the shock
    -> remaining imbalance is cleared mainly by price
```

The inverse case also matters: when inventories are large, spare capacity exists, or supply can respond quickly, the same demand shock should create a smaller price response.

## Day 1 task

Use crude oil and copper as the two examples. Do not begin with a large statistical model.

For each commodity, answer:

1. What physically limits short-run supply adjustment?
2. What inventory or spare-capacity buffer exists?
3. What changes over a longer horizon that makes supply more elastic?
4. Which observable datasets could represent price, production, and inventories?
5. What evidence would falsify the simple mechanism above?
